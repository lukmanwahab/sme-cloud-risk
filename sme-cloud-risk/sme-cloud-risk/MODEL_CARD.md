# Model Card: SME Cloud Security Risk Classifier

Following the structure of Mitchell et al. (2019), "Model Cards for Model Reporting".

## Model details

- **Developer:** Lukman Wahab
- **Version:** 1.0.0 (seed 42)
- **Type:** L2-regularised logistic regression (C = 0.042, chosen by nested randomised search) inside a scikit-learn pipeline with median/mode imputation, standardisation and one-hot sector encoding. Random forest and histogram gradient boosting were benchmarked and were not statistically better.
- **Training data:** 2,250 simulated SMEs (75% stratified split of 3,000). See "Data" below.
- **Licence:** MIT.

## Intended use

Rank small and medium-sized enterprises by their predicted probability of a cloud security incident in the next 12 months, and prioritise which missing security control each firm should adopt first. The intended users are security advisors, SME support programmes and the firms themselves, through a dashboard. The model is a triage and prioritisation aid.

**Out of scope:** insurance pricing, regulatory compliance decisions, or any use where a firm would be penalised on the basis of the score. The model has not been validated for firms with more than 249 employees or outside the eight surveyed sectors.

## Data

The repository ships a **synthetic** survey generated by `cloudrisk/simulate.py`. The original survey data from the CISST study at Cardiff Metropolitan University is confidential and is not included. The simulator encodes a documented causal structure: firm size, IT staffing and cloud maturity drive control adoption, and firm characteristics, control adoption and an MFA x BYOD interaction drive incident risk. Item non-response is injected at 4% in the control questions. All results below are on synthetic data and should be read as a demonstration of the method, not as findings about real SMEs.

## Metrics

Hold-out set of 750 firms, incident prevalence 28%. Percentile bootstrap 95% CIs from 1,000 resamples.

| Metric | Estimate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| ROC-AUC | 0.760 | 0.725 to 0.797 |
| Average precision | 0.557 | 0.490 to 0.625 |
| Brier score | 0.167 | 0.153 to 0.182 |

Paired bootstrap AUC differences: selected model minus random forest +0.013 (CI -0.003 to +0.029, not significant); minus gradient boosting +0.027 (CI +0.010 to +0.044).

Nested 5-fold CV ROC-AUC on the training set: 0.748 ± 0.009, consistent with the hold-out estimate, which indicates no selection optimism.

**Operating point.** With a 5:1 cost ratio for missed incidents versus unnecessary interventions, the cost-minimising threshold is 0.18, giving recall 0.90, precision 0.38 and 65% of firms flagged. The decision curve shows positive net benefit over both "intervene everywhere" and "intervene nowhere" across thresholds from about 0.28 to 0.75; below 0.28 intervening everywhere is as good, which is expected at 28% prevalence.

## Subgroup performance

| Group | n | AUC | Calibration gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro (1 to 9 employees) | 222 | 0.72 | +0.02 |
| Small (10 to 49) | 440 | 0.77 | -0.02 |
| Medium (50 to 249) | 88 | 0.74 | +0.02 |
| Retail | 82 | 0.64 | +0.10 |
| Professional services | 91 | 0.85 | -0.05 |

Discrimination is weakest in retail (AUC 0.64) and the model over-states retail risk by about 10 percentage points. On real data this would warrant either a sector interaction term or a sector-specific recalibration before deployment. Size bands are well calibrated.

## Explanations

Feature attributions use SHAP (LinearExplainer for the selected model, TreeExplainer for the ensembles). Odds ratios with Wald 95% CIs come from the logistic model. The top control gaps on synthetic data, ranked by SHAP impact weighted by the share of firms lacking the control, are least-privilege access, patching SLA, endpoint protection, MFA enforcement and annual security training. These match the five largest protective effects built into the simulator, which is the test in `tests/test_pipeline.py::test_control_gap_ranking_recovers_strong_effects`.

## Counterfactual recommendations

`intervene.py` scores each firm with one absent control switched on and recommends the control with the largest predicted risk reduction. **These are predictive, not causal, estimates.** The model is observational; firms that adopt a control differ from firms that do not in ways the covariates only partly capture. Treat the recommendation as a prioritisation aid for a conversation with the firm, not as the expected effect of the intervention.

## Ethical considerations and limitations

- Scores reflect survey self-report; firms may misreport their controls.
- The synthetic data cannot reproduce real-world distribution shift, adversarial behaviour or emerging threats. Retrain and re-validate on current field data before any operational use.
- Lower discrimination for micro firms and some sectors means ranked lists could systematically under-serve those groups; monitor subgroup metrics after deployment.
- Item non-response is used as a feature. Firms should be told that skipping questions affects their score.

## Reproducibility

`cloudrisk run` regenerates every number in this card from seed 42 in about two and a half minutes. CI runs the test suite and an untuned smoke run on every push.
